## Concepts covered

- A successful `kill` reports signal-request acceptance, while a later `ps`
  snapshot provides separate evidence about whether a PID is present.
- A returning `SIGTERM` handler lets interrupted execution continue.
- `SIGKILL` cannot be caught or ignored and gives the process no application
  cleanup opportunity.
- Safe escalation is conditional: verify identity, send `SIGTERM`, wait,
  inspect, send `SIGKILL` only if still present, wait, then inspect again.
- `ps` is a short-lived snapshot command; `top` stays in the foreground and
  displays discrete, repeated refreshes rather than continuous omniscience.
- On a four-worker capacity model, one fully busy worker uses one quarter of
  total capacity.
- A process need not load an entire opened file into physical RAM; virtual
  address space versus resident and shared pages remains the next lesson.

## Continuation — sampling and `ps %CPU`

### Focus

Complete the discrete-refresh foundation and begin deriving process CPU
percentage from the Ubuntu VM's installed procps-ng documentation.

### Concepts covered

- A `top` frame is the process list shown at one refresh instant; the displayed
  list does not continuously change between refreshes.
- A process that starts and exits entirely between two refreshes can be absent
  from both displayed frames.
- In procps-ng `ps`, process `%CPU` is CPU time used divided by elapsed real
  time since the process started, expressed as a percentage. It is therefore a
  lifetime average rather than an instantaneous reading.
- Dividing seconds by seconds produces a unitless ratio; multiplying that ratio
  by 100 converts it to a percentage.

  that zero refreshes occurred during a process lifetime from `t=0.5` to
  `t=1.5` when refreshes occurred only at `t=0` and `t=2`.
- Verified on Ubuntu that both `ps` and `top` are from procps-ng 4.0.4.
- Located the installed `ps(1)` CPU description and identified CPU time and
  elapsed process lifetime as the numerator and denominator.
- Correctly calculated `5/20` as `1/4` and later independently converted
  `15/60 = 1/4` to `25%`.
