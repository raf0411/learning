# GOAL

Independently solve LeetCode/HackerRank-style coding assessments for junior or entry-level developer roles, focusing primarily on easy problems and progressing to selected medium problems.

Target preparation window: roughly 1–2 months. Scope and pacing must be calibrated to demonstrated starting knowledge and available study time; this is a planning target, not a mastery guarantee.

## Desired capability

- Understand a problem and its constraints, construct an approach, and explain why it works.
- Identify a suitable data structure or algorithm, break the task into smaller steps, and develop a solution before writing code.
- Write and debug a solution without AI assistance.
- Choose useful test cases, including edge cases, and explain basic time and space costs.
- Reconstruct and adapt learned ideas to unfamiliar problems instead of relying on memorized solutions.

## Success criteria

- Repeatedly solve unfamiliar easy problems across the core topics independently.
- Independently solve selected medium problems that build on those foundations.
- Demonstrate these abilities again after spacing and during assessment-style practice without AI or solution hints.

## Context and constraints

- Fresh graduate seeking a first developer job.
- Wants to rebuild DSA and algorithm understanding from scratch after reporting substantial forgetting.
- Has historically disliked the subject; prioritize manageable steps, practical relevance, and evidence of understanding.
- Preferred programming language: Python.
- Available study time: 5 hours per day, 5 days per week (25 hours weekly).
- No specific assessment date has been established beyond the roughly 1–2 month preparation target.
- Prioritize fundamental DSA and practical junior interview problem solving. Competitive programming and advanced algorithms are outside the immediate priority.

## Learning constraints

- Establish a broader baseline before finalizing the dependency-based roadmap. Previous coursework does not establish current understanding.
- During the diagnostic probe, ask progressively, let the learner reason, and withhold teaching, solutions, and immediate corrections. Use follow-ups to locate gaps; assess advanced topics only when readiness is evident.
- During instruction, use concept → small example → guided problem → independent problem → review of reasoning, within the overall PROBE → PLAN → TEACH → PRACTICE → TEST → UPDATE loop.
- Start mostly with Easy problems. Introduce selected Medium problems after their prerequisites are demonstrated.
- Evaluate reasoning alongside code. Use progressively stronger hints and learner-led bottleneck diagnosis; explain full solutions only after a serious attempt or an explicit request.
- Prioritize reconstructing ideas and applying them to unfamiliar problems over memorized solutions, mechanical pattern recognition, or problem counts.
- Learner is a visual learner, prioritize teaching concepts with visual using diagrams when needed.
